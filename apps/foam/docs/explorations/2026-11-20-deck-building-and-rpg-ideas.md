## Starting point

- Inside a night the player manages minutes, money, drunkenness, dice odds and the quest's
  deadlines.
- Across nights the game keeps the collection, past nights and texts read. None of it changes how
  the next night plays.
- The shelved "long arc across nights" reopens after phase 8, which is built.

## How this is worked

- This is gathering, not choosing: ideas are developed, not narrowed to options.
- Ideas come as whole loops with a night played through them, not as lists of parts.
- Think outside the prototype: its places, quest and numbers are not the frame.
- A rule earns its place by the decisions it creates. Chores (the toilet, the locker) and random
  penalties (fumbling, rain, theft) create none.
- What works: deck-building proper (cards, upgrades, combinations) from one stated worldview, with
  example cards.

## Questions and answers

1. Which job do cards and traits do: make the next night differ, make choices inside a night harder,
   or give the player an identity? **All three.**
2. Which of seven loops fit? **Two faces and pockets.** The repertoire, the agenda, habits and
   reputation, the ending pays and the night's deal are not a fit.
3. Two faces and pockets grown into fumbling, hands, pocket kinds, junk, rain, body cards, the
   outfit over the night, a tab against the jacket, lockers, pawning, cash and battery? **Awful, all
   of it.**
4. Two faces and pockets joined into one core (cards lend Nerve, Charm or Eye)? **No: too tied to
   the prototype.** Each round brings new concepts, not extensions of agreed ones.
5. Sixteen high concepts (memories as the deck, friends as the deck, dignity as health, and more)?
   **Incoherent, no good ideas.** Wanted: deck-building itself, meaning cards, upgrades and
   combinations, from one coherent view of the design.
6. The deck-building design (worldview, what a card can do, kinds, getting, upgrades, losing, across
   nights, styles)? **Good.**

## Loops

### Two faces: drunkenness turns the cards over

- Some cards have a sober face and a drunk face; past a level the other face counts.
- "Shy" notices more; "Brave" talks to anyone and is taken seriously by nobody.
- Drunkenness becomes a dial: one drink before the bouncer, a coffee before the ticket machine.
- A face opens different choices; it is never simply better.
- The night has an arc: sober hand early, turned hand at midnight, turned back at dawn as the hours
  lower the level.

### Pockets: the hand has a size

- Cards and the quest's things share the pockets.
- The outfit sets how many: a bathrobe 2, a jacket 4, a backpack 6 (bouncers dislike backpacks).
- The birthday bottle takes room all evening, so the quest crowds out the night's finds.
- With full pockets, taking something new means letting something go.

## A deck-building design

### Worldview

- The deck is how you go out: your moves, habits, people and things. Not stats.
- You hold your cards; you do not draw them. A night has few cards (start 1 to 5, gain 3 to 5), so
  each one matters, and a text game has no turns to draw on.
- Pockets limit how many you hold.
- Every card does one of seven things (below), so content tags its choices by kind and never names a
  card.
- Upgrades change what a card does, not only its numbers.
- Cards combine, so each night's hand becomes a style.
- Playing a card writes a line in the night's journal.

### Card anatomy

- Name, kind (Talk, Drink, Move, Look, Night, People), effect, uses (always, once a place, once a
  night), cost when played (minutes, Kč or a drink), level.

### What a card can do

- Open: adds a choice to every scene of a kind ("[Lighter] Offer a light" wherever people smoke).
- Bend: changes a number on a kind of choice (every talk takes 10 minutes less).
- Bypass: lets you take a choice you are barred from (a shut door, an empty wallet).
- Tilt: odds up, a reroll, or the better of two rolls.
- Trigger: when something happens, something else does ("Each tram ride: +10% on your next talk").
- Transform: changes your other cards ("Wingman: your Talk cards count as People too").
- Generate: makes one-use cards ("Party animal: every bar you enter gives a Dance card").

### Kinds of card

- Moves: active, played into a scene.
- Habits: passive rule benders, always on.
- Things: charges that run out (a lighter, 3 uses).
- People: join you for a few places and lend their cards while they stay.
- Burdens: bad cards that take a pocket (a hangover, a debt) until cleared.
- Stories: every found story is a passive card in the collection, never upgraded. (note)

### Getting cards

- After a notable scene: pick one of three, from that place's own pool, so the route shapes the
  hand.
- A person you get to know gives you their signature move.
- Buy one at a place: a bar teaches a toast.
- A bad outcome gives a burden.

### Upgrades

- Practice: a card played often enough in one night levels up ("Small talk" becomes "Good talk").
- Branching: level 2 picks a direction ("Small talk" becomes "Gossip" or "Flirt").
- Stamps: a reward stamps one card with a rider: Night (stronger after midnight), Echo (comes back
  once), Cheap (costs less), Lucky (odds up), Shared (works for a person with you too).
- Merge: two copies become one stronger card and free a pocket.
- Places that teach: a place upgrades its own kind (a music bar, the Music cards).
- Wear: the same trick in the same place dulls; people have heard the joke.

### Losing cards

- At dawn the night's cards go; you keep one, with its level and stamps.
- Give a card away: it leaves the hand and buys a favour.
- Burdens clear by the act that fits them.

### Across nights

- The collection holds kept cards and stories; you choose the starting hand from it. (note)
- A found story adds new cards to the places' pools.
- A kept card is the long run's progress.

### Styles that come out of combinations

- The talker: "Small talk" (opens a talk with anyone), "Second wind" (after three talks in one
  place, the next is free), "Wingman" (a person: talks +15% while they stay).
- The wanderer: "Shortcut" (walks take a quarter less), "First time" (a place new tonight gives one
  card from its pool), "Night bus" (a tram ride after the last tram).
- The generous one: "Buy a round" (150 Kč, the whole table warms up), "Big tipper" (each payment
  over 100 Kč: +10% on the next roll), "Broke but happy" (at 0 Kč talks cost no minutes).
- The gambler: "Coin flip" (one reroll a place), "Lucky shirt" (the first lost roll of the night
  counts as won), "All in" (a roll's stakes doubled both ways).

## Deck-building, iteration 2

### Counting kinds

- Some cards scale with how many cards of a kind you hold: "Chatterbox: talks +5% per Talk card".
- Pockets are few, so every hand is a choice between specialising and spreading.

### Rarity

- Common: one plain effect, Open or Bend.
- Uncommon: a Trigger or a condition, the glue of a style.
- Rare: Transform or Generate; it changes how the night plays.
- Signature: one per person, only from getting to know them.
- The later the hour, the rarer the offers: staying out pays in better cards.

### Offers

- Pick one of three: two from the place's pool, one that shares a kind with your hand.
- Skipping an offer gives one practice to a card you hold.

### Stamps: how they are earned

- A found story stamps the card played in the scene that found it.
- How the story happened decides the stamp: after midnight Night, through a won roll Lucky, with a
  person Shared, for almost nothing Cheap, on a second visit Echo.
- One stamp per card; a new one replaces the old, which is a decision.
- New stamps: Early (stronger before 20:00), Loud (works on everyone in the scene), Quiet (a bypass
  nobody notices), Twin (counts as two of its kind), Wild (counts as every kind), Sticky (not lost
  at dawn; rare).

### More cards

- Talk: "Ask around" (common, Open: learn one thing about the place), "Name-dropper" (uncommon,
  Trigger: a talk with someone you know tonight opens one with someone they know), "Storyteller"
  (rare, Transform: your stories count as Talk cards), "The last word" (rare, Tilt: retry one lost
  talk a night).
- Move: "Good shoes" (common, Bend: every walk 10 minutes less), "Window seat" (uncommon, Trigger: a
  tram ride gives +10% on the next Look), "Wanderlust" (rare, Transform: journeys count as practice,
  so cards level while you travel).
- Drink: "Shots for the table" (common, Open), "Tab" (uncommon, Bypass: buy now, pay at the next
  place), "Patron" (rare, Generate: every round you buy brings someone from the table along as a
  one-use People card).
- Luck: "Hot streak" (uncommon, Trigger: +5% for every roll won in a row, reset on a loss), "Loaded
  dice" (rare, Tilt: always the better of two rolls, each roll 5 minutes longer).
- Look: "People-watching" (common, Open: notice one thing in any crowded place), "Eavesdrop"
  (uncommon, Generate: listening to a table gives a one-use rumour), "Read the room" (rare, Tilt:
  the first roll in each place takes the better of two).
- Night: "After hours" (uncommon, Bypass: one shut door after midnight opens), "Sunrise" (rare,
  Trigger: from 05:00 every card costs nothing).

### People and burdens

- A person joins for three places, lends their signature card and leaves at their hour.
- A person met on several nights becomes a friend card, who stays longer.
- Burdens: "Hangover" (starts the next night in a pocket), "Debt" (clears when paid), "Said too
  much" (one place won't talk to you until you apologise).
- Some cards feed on burdens: "Hair of the dog" (rare, Transform: a hangover counts as a Drink
  card).

### The long run: what a kept card becomes

- A kept card gains a notch each night it is played; notches open a second stamp slot, then a
  signature effect.
- Its name grows with its history: "Lighter", "Old lighter", "The lighter from the night of the
  fire", after the story that stamped it.
- Retiring: a kept card leaves the hand for good and adds its kind to every place's pool, turning
  power into breadth.
- The starting hand grows slowly with stories found, from 1 to 5.
- Against power creep: old cards compete with new ones for few pockets, wear stops one card from
  carrying every night, and rarity follows the hour, not progress.

## Ideas

### What a card is

- An item with a few uses: phone, lighter, bag of weed. (note)
- A story card for every found story. (note)
- A regular's card for a bar. (note)
- A person you know: played to bring them into a scene or call in a favour.
- A memory from a past night: played where it happened, it opens a flashback.
- A rumour heard tonight ("Rotor's back door is open after 02:00"): true or not by the seed, gone at
  dawn.
- A habit: a verb that works wherever it fits ("Bum a cigarette", "Buy a round").
- A song: chosen at a jukebox, it sets a scene's mood and odds.
- An outfit, chosen before the night: it changes who lets you in and who talks to you.
- A debt or a favour: someone owes you, or you owe them, until it is settled.
- An intervention by the author: "Second draft" rerolls a die, "Cut" skips a wait. Rare, given on
  the phone.

### One frame for every card

- The phone is the deck: people are contacts, memories are photos, rumours are notes.
- Battery is the cost of playing a phone card; charging it costs time at a bar with a socket.
- A lost or stolen phone (Ideas page) takes the deck away until it is found.
- Or the deck is the author's card index for the novel: every card is a note he wrote about Brno.

### Getting cards

- A found story gives its card, or a pick of a card into the deck. (note)
- Visiting a bar often earns its regular's card; past nights count the visits. (note)
- People give cards as a scene's outcome: a number, a tip, a lighter.
- A draft at dawn: the log screen offers one of three, drawn from what happened that night.
- Failure teaches: the stranded ending gives "The 08:04 runs late on Fridays".
- Trading: your lighter for their number.
- Buying: a tram pass, or a round that makes a friend.
- The author sends one on the phone.

### Playing cards in a night

- A key: holding the card adds a choice to a scene. (note)
- An override: the card unlocks a choice you are otherwise barred from (too drunk, too poor, a shut
  door). (note)
- Dice: a card raises the odds or rerolls. (note)
- Spent: uses run out. Passive: a bonus while held. (note)
- A hand limit: pockets hold a few cards, so a new one means dropping one.
- Combos: two cards open what neither does alone (the guitarist's number plus Rotor's regular card
  gets you invited to play).
- Hours: some cards work only at some hours ("Night owl" after 01:00).
- Wear: some cards weaken over the night (charm against drunkenness, a rumour that expires).

### Between nights

- Pick 1 to 5 cards to take into the night (note); the train ride is the moment ("what's in your
  pockets?").
- The outfit sets how many fit: a bathrobe holds 2, a jacket 4.
- Or the seed deals the start hand from the deck: pick one, draw the rest.
- Some cards level up through use (a regular's card I to III); story cards never do. (note)
- A card changes through a story: the lighter used at the birthday becomes "The birthday lighter".
- Cooling: a person's card fades after nights apart; a regular's card lapses.
- Breadth over power: a new card adds scenes to later nights rather than lowering prices.
- Traces: what a night leaves stays in the world (the jacket left at Rotor is there next night).
- A harder night by choice: take a bad card on purpose (a heatwave, 100 Kč less) for a better draft.

### Bad cards

- Hangover: a heavy night puts a card in the next night's hand (slower walking, drinks hit harder).
- Debt: borrowed money becomes "You owe Petr 200" until it is repaid.
- A ban: thrown out of Rotor means "Banned from Rotor" until it is earned back.
- Each bad card brings scenes of its own (repaying the debt is a scene), not only a penalty.

### Traits

- A perk tree instead of many stats, or a few core traits raised by perks. (note)
- Tags instead of numbers ("Smoker", "Talks too much"), read by content as drunkenness is.
- Double-edged traits: "Talks too much" opens more dialogue but every talk costs 10 more minutes.
- A flashback decides who you were and sets a trait.
- The perk tree is the map: local knowledge per district (Husovice: shortcuts on foot).
- The first talk on the train ("Why Brno?") sets the starting traits.
- More body meters (tiredness, hunger, being high) from the Ideas page; traits set how fast they
  move.

### The author and the meta voice

- Between nights the author edits your deck ("I cut this card, it was too strong"): a content update
  told in fiction.
- The author comments on your hand by phone.
- The jukebox (Ideas page) deals song cards and argues with the author about them.

### Presentation

- A card is a title and one line; it needs no art.
- The hand opens from the bottom row, beside Phone.
- A choice a card opens carries the card's name: "[Lighter] Offer a light".

## Thoughts and risks

- Every card earns its place in the loop; no system for its own sake. (note)
- Every card needs a name that makes sense in the fiction. (note)
- Content cost: a key card needs text in every scene that answers it; a number modifier needs none.
- The checker: every hand multiplies the states, so a scene should read few cards.
- The hand has to fit a phone screen about 32 letters across.
- A card that lowers a cost takes away tension; one that opens something adds text to write. A card
  that improves one number and worsens another turns into a resource decision itself.

Clashes with `direction.md`:

- Seed: a player's own deck makes one seed play differently for two players.
- Variation, "no pool, no table": a dealt hand or a draft is a pool.
- Phone: the player never starts a conversation, so calling a person's card breaks the rule.
- Meta jokes are used sparingly, and an index-card frame keeps the meta on screen.
- Drugs are never romanticised, so a weed card with a bonus needs a written cost.
- Money never goes below zero, and a debt card works around that on purpose.
- Drunkenness, "written, not ruled": a card that turns over at a level is a rule.
