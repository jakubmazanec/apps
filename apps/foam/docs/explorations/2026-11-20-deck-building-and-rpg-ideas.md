## Starting point

- Inside a night the player manages minutes, money, drunkenness, dice odds and the quest's
  deadlines.
- Across nights the game keeps the collection, past nights and texts read. None of it changes how
  the next night plays.
- The shelved "long arc across nights" reopens after phase 8, which is built.

## How this is worked

- This is gathering, not choosing: ideas are developed, not narrowed to options.
- Ideas come as whole loops with a night played through them, not as lists of parts.
- What lands (my reading of the kept loops): cards made of the body and of real things carried,
  built on what the night already has, adding tension rather than progress.

## Questions and answers

1. Which job do cards and traits do: make the next night differ, make choices inside a night harder,
   or give the player an identity? **All three.**
2. Which of seven loops fit? **Two faces and pockets.** The repertoire, the agenda, habits and
   reputation, the ending pays and the night's deal are not a fit.

## Loops

### Two faces: drunkenness turns the cards over

- Some cards have a sober face and a drunk face; past a level the other face counts.
- "Shy" notices more; "Brave" talks to anyone and is taken seriously by nobody.
- "Careful with money" turns into "Buying rounds".
- Drunkenness becomes a dial: one drink before the bouncer, a coffee before the ticket machine.
- A face opens different choices; it is never simply better.
- The night has an arc: sober hand early, turned hand at midnight, turned back at dawn as the hours
  lower the level.
- Up and down differ: at the same level a card reads "Brave" on the way up and "Sluggish" on the way
  down.
- Tiredness is a second axis after 03:00; coffee pushes one way, drinks the other.
- The room has a level too: a bar gets drunker through the night, "Brave" works on a drunk room,
  "Shy" on a sober one.
- Sober you chooses, drunk you draws: past a level, reaching into a pocket brings out whatever is
  there.

### Pockets and hands: everything carried takes room

- Cards, the bottle, the keys and bought things share the pockets.
- The outfit sets the room: a bathrobe 2, a jacket 4, a backpack 6 (bouncers dislike backpacks).
- The birthday bottle takes room all evening, so the quest crowds out the night's finds.
- The two hands are the hand: only what is in a hand can be used now; pockets hold the rest.
- Taking something out takes a minute; putting a drink down frees a hand.
- Pockets differ: front is quick, back is quick and easy to pick, inside is a minute slower and safe
  from theft and rain, a bag is roomy and takes a hand.
- Drunk fumbling: past a level, reaching brings out a random thing from that pocket, with shown odds
  of dropping it.
- A dropped thing stays on that place's floor for the night; someone may take it, by the seed.
- Loss and theft stay hidden until you reach for the thing or check your pockets (a minute).
- A full hand forces a drop when something new is offered.
- The Whisky Shop's paper bag: more room, one hand less.
- Junk fills pockets: flyers, receipts, a coaster. Some junk is useful: a napkin with a number, a
  flyer that is an invitation.
- Rain soaks paper in outside pockets: a ticket, the napkin.

### Body cards: needs that take a hand

- The body puts cards in your hands: "Need the toilet" after drinks, "Hungry" hours after eating,
  "Hot" in the heat with the jacket on, "Cold" at dawn without it, "Tired" after 03:00.
- A body card holds a hand, your attention, until it is dealt with: the station's paid toilet, a
  kebab for 15 minutes.
- They are the night's bad cards, made of the body, and they leave when dealt with.

### The outfit over the night

- The jacket's worth changes with the hour: too hot at 19:00, needed at 04:00.
- Taken off, it is carried (a hand) or tied round the waist (its pockets out of reach).
- Left on a chair, it frees you and is at risk.

### Room traded for money and time

- A tab against the jacket: the bar keeps it, and its pockets, until you pay before closing.
- The main station's luggage lockers: pay, leave the bottle, fetch it before the birthday; a detour
  each way.
- Pawn the phone for cash: no phone and no messages until it is bought back.

### Cash and paying by phone

- Money splits: cash in a pocket, and paying by phone.
- Some places take cash only.
- Cash can be dropped or stolen; paying by phone needs battery and a free hand.
- The cash machine costs a fee and the walk to it.
- The battery is a second clock: the map, payments and messages drain it; a power bank takes a
  pocket.

### A night through these loops

- 16:40, the train, in a jacket: phone and lighter in front, cash inside, tram ticket at the back.
- 17:30, The Whisky Shop: the bottle comes in a bag, one hand gone.
- 19:00, the heat: "Hot" takes the other hand, and the phone buzzes unanswered. Off with the jacket,
  or the bottle into a station locker.
- 22:00, Rotor: the bottle is given, a hand frees; three drinks turn "Careful with money" into
  "Buying rounds".
- 01:30, Rotor's door: at 4.5 drinks, reaching for the lighter brings out the tram ticket, which
  falls to the floor.
- 04:00, the level falling: "Cold" arrives, and the jacket is on a chair in Rotor, closed since
  03:00.
- 07:30, the hall: the cash was in the inside pocket, so the ticket can be bought.

## Ideas

### What a card is

- An item with a few uses: phone, lighter, bag of weed. (note)
- A story card for every found story. (note)
- A regular's card for a bar. (note)
- A person you know: played to bring them into a scene or call in a favour.
- A memory from a past night: played where it happened, it opens a flashback.
- A rumour heard tonight ("Rotor's back door is open after 02:00"): true or not by the seed, gone at
  dawn.
- A habit: a verb that works wherever it fits ("Bum a cigarette", "Buy a round").
- A song: chosen at a jukebox, it sets a scene's mood and odds.
- An outfit, chosen before the night: it changes who lets you in and who talks to you.
- A debt or a favour: someone owes you, or you owe them, until it is settled.
- An intervention by the author: "Second draft" rerolls a die, "Cut" skips a wait. Rare, given on
  the phone.

### One frame for every card

- The phone is the deck: people are contacts, memories are photos, rumours are notes.
- Battery is the cost of playing a phone card; charging it costs time at a bar with a socket.
- A lost or stolen phone (Ideas page) takes the deck away until it is found.
- Or the deck is the author's card index for the novel: every card is a note he wrote about Brno.

### Getting cards

- A found story gives its card, or a pick of a card into the deck. (note)
- Visiting a bar often earns its regular's card; past nights count the visits. (note)
- People give cards as a scene's outcome: a number, a tip, a lighter.
- A draft at dawn: the log screen offers one of three, drawn from what happened that night.
- Failure teaches: the stranded ending gives "The 08:04 runs late on Fridays".
- Trading: your lighter for their number.
- Buying: a tram pass, or a round that makes a friend.
- The author sends one on the phone.

### Playing cards in a night

- A key: holding the card adds a choice to a scene. (note)
- An override: the card unlocks a choice you are otherwise barred from (too drunk, too poor, a shut
  door). (note)
- Dice: a card raises the odds or rerolls. (note)
- Spent: uses run out. Passive: a bonus while held. (note)
- A hand limit: pockets hold a few cards, so a new one means dropping one.
- Combos: two cards open what neither does alone (the guitarist's number plus Rotor's regular card
  gets you invited to play).
- Hours: some cards work only at some hours ("Night owl" after 01:00).
- Wear: some cards weaken over the night (charm against drunkenness, a rumour that expires).

### Between nights

- Pick 1 to 5 cards to take into the night (note); the train ride is the moment ("what's in your
  pockets?").
- The outfit sets how many fit: a bathrobe holds 2, a jacket 4.
- Or the seed deals the start hand from the deck: pick one, draw the rest.
- Some cards level up through use (a regular's card I to III); story cards never do. (note)
- A card changes through a story: the lighter used at the birthday becomes "The birthday lighter".
- Cooling: a person's card fades after nights apart; a regular's card lapses.
- Breadth over power: a new card adds scenes to later nights rather than lowering prices.
- Traces: what a night leaves stays in the world (the jacket left at Rotor is there next night).
- A harder night by choice: take a bad card on purpose (a heatwave, 100 Kč less) for a better draft.

### Bad cards

- Hangover: a heavy night puts a card in the next night's hand (slower walking, drinks hit harder).
- Debt: borrowed money becomes "You owe Petr 200" until it is repaid.
- A ban: thrown out of Rotor means "Banned from Rotor" until it is earned back.
- Each bad card brings scenes of its own (repaying the debt is a scene), not only a penalty.

### Traits

- A perk tree instead of many stats, or a few core traits raised by perks. (note)
- Tags instead of numbers ("Smoker", "Talks too much"), read by content as drunkenness is.
- Double-edged traits: "Talks too much" opens more dialogue but every talk costs 10 more minutes.
- A flashback decides who you were and sets a trait.
- The perk tree is the map: local knowledge per district (Husovice: shortcuts on foot).
- The first talk on the train ("Why Brno?") sets the starting traits.
- More body meters (tiredness, hunger, being high) from the Ideas page; traits set how fast they
  move.

### The author and the meta voice

- Between nights the author edits your deck ("I cut this card, it was too strong"): a content update
  told in fiction.
- The author comments on your hand by phone.
- The jukebox (Ideas page) deals song cards and argues with the author about them.

### Presentation

- A card is a title and one line; it needs no art.
- The hand opens from the bottom row, beside Phone.
- A choice a card opens carries the card's name: "[Lighter] Offer a light".

## Thoughts and risks

- Every card earns its place in the loop; no system for its own sake. (note)
- Every card needs a name that makes sense in the fiction. (note)
- Content cost: a key card needs text in every scene that answers it; a number modifier needs none.
- The checker: every hand multiplies the states, so a scene should read few cards.
- Two hands and a few pockets have to fit a phone screen about 32 letters across.
- A card that lowers a cost takes away tension; one that opens something adds text to write. A card
  that improves one number and worsens another turns into a resource decision itself.

Clashes with `direction.md`:

- Seed: a player's own deck makes one seed play differently for two players.
- Variation, "no pool, no table": a dealt hand or a draft is a pool.
- Phone: the player never starts a conversation, so calling a person's card breaks the rule.
- Meta jokes are used sparingly, and an index-card frame keeps the meta on screen.
- Drugs are never romanticised, so a weed card with a bonus needs a written cost.
- Money never goes below zero, and a debt card works around that on purpose.
- Drunkenness, "written, not ruled": a card that turns over at a level is a rule.
